Resistance is DC. The gas sets how well the conductor is cooled, cooling sets its temperature, and temperature sets resistivity — which is why the same cable drops more volts in vacuum than in gas at the same current.

Heat leaves by radiation and through the gas: conduction across the annulus, corrected for temperature jump so it falls away with pressure once the mean free path rivals the gap, plus natural convection once the Rayleigh number supports it. Gas properties are scaled from 300 K values.
